Millionaire for Life: $1 Million a Year or $18 Million in Cash?

The top prize in Millionaire for Life is $1 million a year for life, or a single $18 million cash payment. The headline makes the annuity look far bigger over time, but the tax math narrows the gap. Here is how it works for a single filer using 2026 federal brackets.

The yearly payment. A $1,000,000 payment, less the $16,100 standard deduction, is taxed through the brackets and comes to about $320,000 in federal tax. That leaves $680,000 a year. Because the prize is the only income in this example, most of the payment lands in the 37% bracket.

The cash option. An $18 million lump sum is taxed at the same schedule, and almost all of it falls in the 37% bracket. Federal tax is about $6.61 million, which leaves roughly $11.39 million. That is the real amount to compare against $680,000 a year.

Break-even. Divide $680,000 by $11.39 million and you get 5.97%. If you could invest the cash and earn a steady 5.97% after tax, the investment would throw off exactly what the annuity pays and your principal would stay untouched. A realistic safe return is lower, say 4%. At 4% the $11.39 million produces about $455,600 a year, well under $680,000. The annuity wins on pure income, though the lump sum still grows your estate and leaves a legacy.

Longevity check. Over 30 years the annuity pays $20.4 million after tax before any investing. Over 20 years it pays $13.6 million, which is already more than the cash. The cash only wins if you expect to use it for growth that beats the payment stream, or if you value control and inheritance.

Don't forget the state. The numbers above leave out state income tax. A winner in a state with a 5% top rate would lose another $50,000 a year on the annuity, or $900,000 on the lump sum, so compare your own state on the lottery tax by state page before deciding.

The bottom line. The annuity delivers more guaranteed after-tax income, while the cash gives flexibility. The break-even yield near 6% is the key number to remember.
