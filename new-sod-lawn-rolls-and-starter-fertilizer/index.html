New Sod Lawn: How Many Rolls to Buy and How Much Starter Fertilizer

Laying sod is the fastest way to a green yard, and a little arithmetic keeps you from ordering too much or running short in the middle of the job. This example covers a back lawn of 40 ft by 35 ft with a flower bed cut out of one corner.

Step 1: Measure the area

The rectangle is 40 x 35 = 1,400 square feet. The flower bed is a 10 ft by 8 ft rectangle, which is 80 square feet. The net lawn area is 1,320 square feet.

Step 2: Count the rolls

Sod is usually sold in rolls or slabs of 10 square feet (2 ft by 5 ft), and pallets typically hold 400 to 500 square feet. Add 5 to 10 percent for trimming around curves.

  • Area with 7 percent waste: 1,320 x 1.07 = 1,412 square feet
  • Rolls at 10 sq ft: 142 rolls
  • Pallets at 450 sq ft: about 3.1, so order 3 pallets and a few extra rolls

The sod calculator lets you enter irregular shapes and returns rolls and pallets directly.

Step 3: Starter fertilizer

New sod roots best with a starter fertilizer high in phosphorus, such as a 10-20-10 product. A common rate is 1 pound of actual nitrogen per 1,000 square feet.

  • Pounds of product = 1 / 0.10 = 10 lb per 1,000 square feet
  • For 1,320 square feet: 13.2 lb of product

A 15 lb bag covers it, with a little to spare. If you want to use a different analysis, the fertilizer calculator converts the nitrogen target into pounds of product for any N-P-K ratio.

Step 4: Laying and watering

Prepare the soil first: till the top 4 inches, rake it level, and roll lightly so footprints do not leave low spots. Lay the first row along a straight edge, stagger the seams like bricks, and press the edges tight without stretching the pieces.

  • Water within 30 minutes of laying each section.
  • Keep the sod damp, not soggy, for the first two weeks.
  • Stay off the lawn for 10 to 14 days while roots take hold.
  • Wait for the first mowing until the sod resists a gentle tug.

Timing

Early fall and spring are the best times to lay sod. In hot summer weather, order sod for the day you will install it and never leave rolls stacked in the sun.

Summary

For this yard you need about 142 rolls or 3 pallets plus extras, and a single 15 lb bag of 10-20-10 starter fertilizer. Do the numbers once, add a small waste margin, and the job goes much faster.
