Retaining Wall Prep: How Much Dirt to Dig Out and How Much Block You Need

A short retaining wall looks easy until you realize how much soil has to move. Here is a worked example for a 20 ft wall, 3 ft high above grade, built with 8 in by 16 in segmental blocks.

Step 1: Count the blocks

Each block covers 8 x 16 = 128 square inches, or 0.89 square feet, of wall face.

  • Wall face: 20 ft x 3 ft = 60 square feet
  • Blocks: 60 / 0.89 = 68
  • Add the buried base course (one extra row, 15 blocks) and 5 percent waste: about 87 blocks

For a quick check on other wall sizes, the retaining wall calculator estimates blocks, cap stones and drainage gravel.

Step 2: Dig the trench

The base trench should be about 24 in wide for a block that is 12 in deep, so there is room for compacted gravel and drainage behind the wall. The depth is 6 in of gravel plus the 8 in buried course, or 14 in.

  • Trench volume: 20 ft x 2 ft x 1.17 ft = 46.7 cubic feet
  • Behind-the-wall excavation if you also need room for drainage stone: 20 x 1 x 3 = 60 cubic feet

Together that is about 107 cubic feet, or 4 cubic yards of loose soil in place. Soil swells by roughly 20 to 25 percent when dug, so plan on hauling or spreading about 5 cubic yards. To convert area and depth into cubic yards and tons for disposal, the dirt calculator is handy.

Step 3: Drainage gravel

Water pressure is what makes retaining walls fail. Backfill the first foot behind the wall with clean drainage stone.

  • Volume: 20 ft x 1 ft x 3 ft = 60 cubic feet, about 2.2 cubic yards
  • Add the 6 in base layer: 20 x 2 x 0.5 = 20 cubic feet

You need roughly 3 cubic yards of crushed stone in total, and a perforated drain pipe at the base that runs to daylight.

Step 4: Build tips

  • Compact the base in thin layers with a plate compactor.
  • Check level along the length and front to back for every course.
  • Step the wall back slightly (a setback of about 1 inch per course) for stability.
  • Walls over about 4 feet often need an engineer, a permit, or both.

Summary

For this wall expect about 87 blocks, around 5 cubic yards of soil to move, and 3 cubic yards of drainage stone. Having these totals before the day you start means you can book the delivery, the dumpster and a helper at the same time, and you will not be driving back to the yard with the trench half dug.
