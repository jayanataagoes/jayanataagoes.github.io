A $500 Million Mega Millions Jackpot: What the Annuity, the Cash Option and the IRS Leave You

A $500 million Mega Millions jackpot sounds like a $500 million check. It is not. The advertised figure is the total of a 30-year annuity, and almost nobody collects it that way. Here is the arithmetic for a single winner, using 2026 federal brackets.

The cash option. For a $500 million advertised jackpot the cash value is typically a bit under half, so assume $230 million. That is the pre-tax number you actually compare against the annuity.

Federal tax. After the $16,100 standard deduction, a single filer's $230 million lands almost entirely in the 37% bracket. The full bill works out to about $85.05 million, which leaves roughly $144.95 million. The lottery withholds only a flat 24% up front, or $55.2 million, so the winner still owes about $29.85 million when the return is filed. Setting that money aside on day one is the single most useful habit a new winner can have.

The annuity side. Mega Millions annuities are 30 payments, each growing 5% over the last. On $500 million the first payment is roughly $7.5 million and the thirtieth about $31 million. Each payment is taxed in the year it arrives, so early payments are hit at 37% above $640,600 of income and the effective rate stays close to that for decades. The annuity trades a lower yearly tax bite for a smaller, spread-out sum, and it protects people who worry about spending it too fast.

Putting it side by side. To see this with your own jackpot and cash value, plug the numbers into the Mega Millions annuity calculator. It lays the 30 payments next to the lump sum so you can judge them after tax. For the one-time cash payout, the Mega Millions tax calculator takes the jackpot, the cash value and the state and returns take-home.

State matters. The $144.95 million above assumes no state income tax, as in Florida or Texas. In a state that takes 6%, the same ticket loses another $13.8 million. Our lottery tax calculator lets you switch states and filing status and watch the number move.

A practical rule. Divide the advertised jackpot by about 2.2 to estimate cash, then multiply cash by roughly 0.63 for federal tax at the top bracket. A $500 million headline becomes about $145 million, and that is the number to plan around.

Always confirm the cash value with the lottery on drawing night, because it moves with interest rates and ticket sales, and talk to a tax professional before you claim.
